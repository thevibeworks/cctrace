# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The trajectory bar's human lane is the TURNS lane now: one clickable block per working loop, from the instant the prompt hit the wire (the block's accent edge, exactly where the point was) to the loop's last reply, numbered the way the rail numbers it. The number shows at any zoom the block can hold it; the prompt's first words wait for full depth. Hover gives the turn's tally - steps, calls, agents spawned, the clock and duration, the prompt, compactions carried and failed requests that fell inside it. Click jumps the conversation to the turn's head (replaying, it seeks where the prompt became visible, as every span does). The block under the conversation's reading position lights as you scroll - the bar-to-convo sync, on the block itself. A harness-started loop is still a turn, drawn faint and saying who started it; subagent loops stay on the agents lane
- The Context view has ONE inspector now: a right panel every deck opens on a pick. Click an icicle node, a stream record or an event row and it opens beside the deck; x or Esc closes it and the deck takes the whole width back. The window deck's pane used to sit UNDER the icicle in the same scroll - a leaf's bytes pushed the graph off the sheet - and the stream's inspector was always there, 40% of the deck spent on "pick a record"; events had no detail at all
- Inside, a vertical rail of facets - the questions the wire can answer about the pick, and only those: content (what it is, rendered as in the requests detail; the picked tool's own fold opens), schema (a tool's declared schema in the request that carried the call: description, input schema, its weight and rank among that request's tools), origin (the step it entered the window with, and the CARRY - how many requests re-sent it and what that weighs; exact through the pinned step for an icicle item, counted up to the next compaction boundary and saying so for a stream record), wire (the carrying request in brief: model, status and stop reason, timing and ttft, prompt by cache read / write / input, output, the step's estimated cost, both links out). A facet that would only say n/a is not listed
- Events open too: an injection shows the injected text itself, a compaction its before -> after by category beside the provider-anchored prompt delta, a cost bump its cause with the TTL gap or the failed previous status, the tokens re-billed and the over-warm estimate. A rolled row says how many occurrences it stands for and shows the most recent
- Esc peels the inspector first, then the range, the zoom, the view. The inspector's scroll survives a live pair and resets when the pick or the facet changes; the picked row stays lit across the repaint
- Fixed blank rows under an icicle tool-result group: an image-bearing result (a screenshot from a browser tool) had no label because only string content was read. The label reads the array now, [image] for the picture
- Data layer: ctxCarrySpan in src/context.ts (pure, inlined, unit-tested) - the carry count behind the origin facet
- Replay holds the reader's thread now. The session rebuilds from the wire as of the cursor, and the selection used to re-resolve against that cursored subset - before the selected thread's first response it fell back to whatever thread existed at the cursor, overwrote the selection, and took the strip's axis with it: the rail flipped to a stranger and the bar re-scaled itself mid-playback. The selection now resolves once against the full capture and pins for the whole replay; a cursor before the thread's first response says "nothing on this thread's wire yet at this moment" instead of switching threads, and the playhead crosses one stable ruler
- Replay enters and restarts on the thread's own edges. On a merged capture the tape can start 44 minutes inside another session and end 8 hours after the one you are reading - entering replay used to park there, the whole strip behind one break column. Enter now parks at the selected thread's last pair (reading pages), restart / Home seek its first; fast-forward / End still mean the tape's live edge
- The arrow keys step this conversation. The turn stepper walks the selected thread's own pairs, so a merged capture's other sessions no longer eat keypresses; shift+arrow keeps every wire boundary
- Waiting folds on the trajectory bar. A call-less gap - the harness sat idle, nobody worked - counted as busy time, so a 45-minute wait held half the strip while the actual work squeezed into the rest. Waiting gaps now stretch the thread's extent but join the compressible idle, and the fold threshold drops from 5 to 2 minutes: the work is the strip, the waits are // breaks with their duration in the clock row
- The trajectory bar is the session view's OVERVIEW now, always on top. The strip - five lanes over wall-clock, the clock ruler, idle folded to // breaks, the selected thread in focus - used to appear only after pressing replay; an overview that must be asked for is not an overview. It now draws on every session render and grows on every landed pair. Replay's own chrome (the transport, the veil, the playhead, the slice band) still waits for replay
- The bar syncs with the session's turns, both ways. A faint reading marker on the strip tracks the topmost turn in the conversation's viewport as you scroll (it moves only under your own scroll, and hides while replaying - the playhead owns position there); clicking the bar jumps the conversation to the turn at that instant. Clicking no longer enters replay - an overview click is navigation, not a mode switch; replay stays on the toolbar button, Space and the arrows
- Removed the loop-row flowchart from the replay bar, with its data layer (loopAt/nowAt/stateAt) and its two animations. It survived three redesigns and one week of real use before the verdict landed: a machine with three states never changes shape, so after one glance the drawing carried no information - the strip, the beat and the conversation already state what is happening at the cursor. The motion budget returns to one owner, the playhead
- Made the bar collapsible: a chevron in the clock gutter cell folds the lanes to the ~13px clock row (a thin ruler is still an overview, and the reopen target), persisted across visits. Replay unfolds it - the instrument needs its lanes. A trace with no session pairs hides the bar entirely

## 0.46.0

- Added COST as a third reading of the Context view, not a fourth tab. The overview grows a cost track beside ctx and time: one column per step (or per turn, same granularity toggle), stacked cache read / cache write / input / output against the thread's dearest column, under the same brush, with the same hover-scrub, click-pin and dimming. The gutter states the top of scale. Measured on a real 1209-pair trace: 74% of the ≈$81 that thread spent was cache READS - re-reading the assembled window on every step - 15% cache writes, 11% output, and fresh input rounded to zero. That is the shape the track exists to show
- Marked the cost BUMPS: a step whose cache hit was under 90% (or cold) after some earlier step had already banked a prefix. Each one wears an amber $ on its column the way a compaction wears a scissors mark, and names its cause off the wire in precedence order - retry (the previous request failed, errored, or was cut off before it completed, so it never banked its write), expired (the gap from the previous request's end exceeded that write's TTL: 1h when it wrote 1h tokens, else 5m, and a pure read refreshes 5m), or prefix changed (with the same-step timeline event that explains it - system prompt, tool schemas, a compaction, a model switch - or an honest "cause not on the wire"). A conversation's first request is a start, not a bump, and a thread that never used the cache reports none
- Priced the counterfactual next to it: "over warm" is what the re-billed tokens (uncached input + cache writes) would have cost had a warm cache read them, at each token class's own rate. On that same trace the main thread carried exactly one bump - a 5% hit on a 341k prompt, ≈$6.48 over warm - and the cause was on the wire: the model changed on that step, which invalidates the whole prefix
- Added two margin blocks. "where the money went" gives the thread's bill by component (bar + key, ≈$ and %), per-model lines when a thread used more than one, "N steps unpriced" when the catalog did not know a model, and a bumps line that is a control: it opens the events deck filtered to cost. "quota" reads the account limits the client polled on the wire (/api/oauth/usage, both the flat five_hour/seven_day shape and the newer limits[]): one row per window with a percent bar, absolute reset time, credits when enabled, and the movement across the trace ("5h 12% -> 37%"). Per trace, not per thread, so it does not repaint on a scrub; clients that never poll (codex/grok/kimi/opencode) render nothing
- Extended three existing surfaces rather than adding new ones: the balance's this-step block leads with "≈$0.42 this step . 97% from cache" (and the bare cache chip stays only when the step is unpriced, so the percentage is stated once), the brushed range caption adds the span's own ≈$, and cost events flow into the events deck with a cost kind chip, the cause in words, and DOLLARS in the delta slot (≈+$1.29) - the hover states the counterfactual in tokens
- Added src/cost.ts - stepCost, threadCostSplit, costEvents, usagePolls - pure, JSON-safe and inlined into the page like context.ts, unit-tested on synthetic pairs (18 tests: the three causes, the first-request and never-cached exclusions, an OpenAI-dialect pair pricing through the same path, the warm arithmetic against the embedded Claude table, both usage shapes). Four new theme variables (--cost-read / --cost-write / --cost-input / --cost-output) as a cool-to-hot ramp, both themes: the cost track must not read as a second composition track
- Consolidated the per-pair cost memo. The header's totals kept their own _cost number beside extractCallInfo's _ci; there is now one memo (_sc, the full component breakdown) and one function that fills it
- Truth boundary unchanged: every dollar is an estimate from catalog rates and renders with a leading ≈, every cause is a wire fact, an unpriced model draws no track / no block / no $0, and quota resets render as absolute wall-clock, never a countdown (docs/design/cost.md)
- Replay TAILS a live session now. Parking at the live edge used to be a lie: a landed pair redrew the strip but never moved the cursor, so a reader who entered replay on a running session fell behind in silence while the beat, the conversation and the state froze at entry time. A pair now measures where the reader was BEFORE it lands - at the edge the cursor follows it, the loop row and the beat move with it, and the conversation sticks to its bottom only if the reader was already there. Behind the edge nothing moves and the live chip flips to a snap-back button. Older pairs merged in from a prior run never move the cursor; playback reaching the end of a live tape parks at the edge instead of just stopping
- Redrew the stage's state diagram as the LOOP ROW in the replay bar - a live flowchart. Rev 1's six nodes and nine counted edges modelled a Markov chain, not Claude Code's loop: reply is an outcome rather than a state, waiting read 0 on every trace inspected, human never lit, the counts were redundant (tools 160 = model>tools 160 = tools>model 159) and the headline restated the conversation header's time chip verbatim. The row draws the actual loop, once, at fixed pixel geometry in the frame under the strip: boxes for human, model and one hand-off slot that reads tools, agents or waiting; a calls? decision diamond (the reply's tool_use blocks - a wire fact); five labelled edges (prompt, yes, results over the row, no -> answer under it). One box lights for the state at the cursor, the edge it came in by lights and FLOWS (an animated dash - the transition in progress), a failed request is the model box in red with no edge, and beside the drawing: the state, what is running, how long it has held where that is a wire fact, and the absolute clock it began at. No counts on it: the tally stays in "so far". Click it to jump to the step that opened the state. At the live edge the model box beats while a request is in flight. prefers-reduced-motion drops the flow and the heartbeat
- The trajectory strip's axis is the SELECTED thread's own time now, with idle folded. It used to be the whole capture: on a 10h38m trace of six sessions the selected 1h34m session sat in the left quarter of the strip and three quarters of the frame drew nothing. The axis now runs from the selected thread's first item to its last (its own spans, points and marks plus the subagents it spawned; other threads still draw ghosted where they fall inside it), and a gap in its busy time over five minutes collapses to a fixed 28px hatched break with the skipped duration in the clock row (// 1h 29m). One mapping (timeScale in src/replay.ts) positions everything - spans, ticks, veil, playhead, slice, drag, wheel zoom - so nothing drifts; the transport's clock and length stay real wall-clock. Changing the selected thread re-rules the axis
- Added a clock axis to the trajectory strip. It had no absolute time anywhere: ticks now label the strip in local wall-clock, coarse enough to stay >= 72px apart at any zoom, aligned to the clock rather than to the capture's start, and the first tick of a calendar day names the date and drops a hairline through every lane
- The FUTURE is what dims now, not the past. The accent-tinted past fill was a player convention that cost the model spans their contrast exactly where the reader looks; a veil covers the strip from the playhead to the right edge instead - the same statement the conversation makes, that a replay never shows you something that has not happened. The playhead gained a halo in the strip's own color and a flag on the clock row, so it reads over a span of any hue
- Thread focus on the strip: the rail's selected thread draws in full and every other thread's spans, points and marks ghost. The model and tools lanes used to draw every thread's pairs on top of each other, so a subagent's requests read as the main loop's
- The conversation follows the cursor. Every seek, step, chapter jump and playback tick now lands on the conversation's bottom - the newest visible turn IS the moment - instantly, never animated; a reader who scrolled up while paused is still never moved
- Transport: added an end button (and the End key) to seek the end of the tape, which on a live run is the live edge and resumes tailing; the clock reads absolute local time before the tape offset, because the terminal the human is looking at shows wall-clock; a live chip states whether the page is tailing or offers the way back; and exit means exit everywhere - it no longer doubles as "back to live"
- The beat names the task it serves: under the caption, the human prompt that started this working loop, click to jump to that chapter. Under the stage, one faint line tallies the calls so far - Bash 157 . Write 2 . ToolSearch 1 . 2 agents . 1 failed - which is the one figure nothing else on the page states
- Data layer: nowAt, soFar and axisTicks land in src/replay.ts (pure, inlined, unit-tested); stateCounts and its transitions table go with the diagram

## 0.45.0

- Replay is a STAGE now, not a bar and a transcript. Entering replay in the Sessions view turns the one-row scrubber into a trajectory: five lanes over wall-clock - the human's prompts, the model's requests, the tools running between them (a harness wait dims in the same lane), subagents as stacked spans from their first request to their last, and the harness lane carrying compaction cuts and failed requests. Same playhead, same shift+drag slice, same deep links; the wheel zooms 1x to 32x around the cursor and the strip says more as you zoom - spans only, then tool initials, then names and the prompt's first words (archify's rule: reading depth is a function of zoom, not a toggle). Click a span to jump to the moment it became visible. Every span is a wire timestamp; the lanes draw exactly the milliseconds threadTimeSplit counts, and a failed or superseded request is spanned, never counted (docs/design/replay-stage.md)
- Added the agent's observed state machine to the stage: a fixed six-node diagram at the top of the left pane while replaying - human -> model -> tools / agents / waiting -> reply -> human - each node and transition carrying its count and time as of the cursor, the current state and the transition just taken lit. Nothing in it is inferred: a transition is a wire fact (a reply with tool_use, a Task call, a stop reason, a loop that ended), never a guess at why. Under it, the BEAT - what the agent did at this step: the turn.step address, the request's clock and stop reason, one row per tool call fused with its result and ok/err, spawn rows linking to the child thread, the reply's first line on a reply step, the model's stated reasoning dimmed and labelled as such, and the window delta. `[` and `]` jump between working loops (the chapters), `F` clears the chrome for presenting, Esc peels present -> replay -> view. Motion budget unchanged: the playhead is the one thing that moves
- Live state. A pair used to reach the page only when its response completed, so the stage could say "the model just thought for 12s" but never "the model is thinking now". Both proxies now mint the pair id BEFORE forwarding and announce a messages-category request as it goes out; the server broadcasts it (`{type:"start"}`), lists open ones in `init.starts` for a page that connects mid-request, and retires it when the pair lands. The strip draws an open request as a dashed span to the newest known time and the diagram lights the model node - no ticking clock, no timers; a snapshot has no starts and says nothing about now. Fail-soft: a throwing hook never touches the forward
- Data layer for the above in src/replay.ts, pure and inlined like session.ts: stepOutcome, sessionLanes, stateAt, stateCounts, beatAt, chaptersOf; threadTimeSplit.byPair now carries the gap's [t0, t1] so one definition of tools/waiting time feeds the context overview and the lanes alike. Unit-tested on synthetic pairs and on a real compaction trace; the strip and the stage are verified in a real browser (test-output/replay-stage/drive.mjs)
- Rebuilt the Context view as a DevTools-shaped shell and folded the Trajectory tab into it. Two tabs for one subject was the error: a reader who had picked a thread and scrubbed to a step lost both by clicking a tab to ask a different question about the same thread. There are now three page tabs - requests, sessions, context - and the context page is a frame (a fixed head, an overview that never scrolls away) around a margin and one deck at a time. #/trajectory still resolves: it lands on the stream deck and rewrites itself to #/context/<key>/=stream
- Made the trajectory bar interactive, the way Chrome DevTools' Performance overview is. It is now the page's time axis and it drives everything under it: hover scrubs the margin and the icicle, click PINS one step, drag BRUSHES a range, the handles resize that range from either edge, dragging the window pans it, the wheel zooms around the cursor (fit to 32x), shift+wheel is left to the browser, Esc peels one layer at a time (range -> zoom -> view), 1/2/3 pick the deck, arrows walk the pin. The brushed range states itself out loud ("23 of 88 selected . turn 04 -> turn 11 . esc clears") - a range that is only a rectangle is a range you have to infer. Everything repaints in place; a full re-render per pointer move would rebuild a 400k-token decomposition sixty times a second
- Two selections that mean different things, which is what lets one page carry three readings without ambiguity: the PIN is one step and drives the margin's balance and the window deck (what was the model carrying at this exact request); the RANGE is a span of steps and scopes the stream and the events (what happened across these requests). The range never touches the balance, because a balance is a thing one request has. The range is stored in step indices, not column indices, so flipping the step/turn granularity redraws the same selection instead of silently meaning something else
- Added a second overview track: where each step's wall-clock went - the request's own duration (model), then the gap to the next request of the same working loop (tools when the reply made calls, waiting when the harness came back on its own). Same x axis, same brush, off threadTimeSplit.byPair, every figure a wire timestamp. This is the per-step waterfall the Trajectory doc listed as a follow-up, and it replaces the strip of thread totals that tab carried on top; the totals and the legend naming the three hues moved to the margin, where standing totals belong. The bar floor is one PIXEL, not a percent, so a 6-second step under a 2m24s outlier still reads as a baseline the spikes rise out of
- Three decks over one selection, picked by a segmented control and addressable as /=window (the default, kept out of the URL), /=stream, /=events: window is the pinned step decomposed as the icicle with its pane; stream is the record stream that shipped as the Trajectory tab in 0.44, unchanged in form and scoped to the brushed range; events is what grew or reclaimed the window, also scoped. Each deck's own controls (the size/order lens, the MAP/READ/FULL level + kind filter + search, the event kind chips) sit on the right of the same bar, with a hint line under it saying what you are looking at. Clicking a ledger line from the stream or the events deck brings the window deck with it - it is a control on the graph, and the margin is beside every deck
- The brushed range slices the stream instead of filtering it by pair. Most of a long spine has no wire pair of its own - the history arrived inside request bodies whose own requests were never captured, or live in a prior trace - so a "does this record belong to a selected step" test dropped 47 rows out of 417 on a real 26-step thread and read as broken. The range is now a contiguous slice of the stream: the first record attributed to its first step through the last attributed to its last, computed over the full record list so it means the same thing at every detail level. Two record kinds gained a wire pair on the way: a user turn and an injection are now attributed to the request that CARRIED them into the window - the same request their Context-pane provenance already names - so their "wire ->" link resolves too
- Overview columns are now equal-width and gapless, with the BAR inside each column capped instead of the column. That is load-bearing: the brush positions its edges at i/N of the track, which is only exact when every column occupies exactly 1/N of it - a 2px gap drifts the overlay by a column width across 100 steps. Two side effects, both wins: a five-step thread reads as five slim bars across the axis instead of a 110px huddle in a 1000px field, and every column keeps a full-width hit target
- The context page is an app shell now, not a scroll: the margin and the deck each scroll themselves, which retires the max-height: calc(100vh - 120px) magic constant the sticky margin needed and lets the stream deck's list and inspector be their own scroll columns. Live re-renders preserve every position - both scrolls, the stream list's scroll and its search box's focus and caret, the overview's horizontal scroll (which still sticks to the newest edge when it was already there), plus the zoom, the brushed range (clamped as steps arrive under it, never dropped), the granularity, the pin, the filters and the fold state
- The brush, the handles, the pan, wheel zoom, the deck switch, the deep links and the hover scrub are verified in a real browser (test-output/ctx-rebuild/drive*.mjs); bun test covers the markup grammar and the routes, since the DOM stub renders markup and cannot dispatch pointer events

## 0.44.0

- Added the Trajectory view - a fourth page tab (#/trajectory) that renders a thread as one linear, time-anchored stream of records: system prompt, the human's turns, the CONTEXT the harness injected (inline, first-class, at the moment it entered), the model's thinking, each tool call fused with its result, the reply - kind-badged, in spine order, with the time lanes (model/tools/waiting/between) as a strip on top and a record inspector beside it. Borrowed from the DeepSeek Harness web UI's Trajectory tab, studied live rather than from source: the move it adds over the Sessions convo is context injections as first-class inline rows woven into the reasoning flow (the "context trajectory"), so you watch context enter the window inline with the reasoning that consumed it. Built from the reconstructed spine (trajectoryRecords in src/context.ts), so it is exact and every record carries its wire pair; turn/step addressing matches the Sessions outline and the Context view. A detail-level toggle borrowed from Archify's progressive disclosure - MAP (system + the human + tool calls), READ (drop the budget banners and bare thinking, keep the substance), FULL (everything) - filters, never summarizes, and states how many rows it hid. A kind filter isolates any one record type (context-only IS the context trajectory), plus search. Two panes like the requests view (record list | inspector); the inspector reuses the detail panel's own block renderers. Pure client-side, works in snapshots. (The earlier Sessions "time" chip borrowed dsh Trajectory's numbers; this is its form - see docs/design/context-view.md, where the Trajectory design was folded in)
- Borrowed dsh Trajectory's time lanes into the Sessions view as a "time" chip: where a thread's wall-clock actually went — model (the requests' own durations), tools (the gap after a reply that made tool calls until the same working loop's next request — one gap covers calls run in parallel), waiting (that gap after a reply with no tool call — the harness came back on its own: a nudge, a loaded tool, a recap), and between turns (the gap before the next prompt arrived). Every figure is a wire timestamp, nothing estimated; the split is in the hover, and each assistant role bar also carries its own step's tools/waiting time beside its duration. dsh reads this off a harness event log with three fixed lanes; cctrace reads it off its own attributed pairs (threadTimeSplit in src/session.ts), so failed and superseded requests are spanned, never counted. Rendered only when the thread has more than one step
- Borrowed semantica's decision-provenance trail into the context graph: every pane row now says which turn first carried that item into the window ("since turn 04 · step 2" for a prompt / tool result / injection, "from turn 07" for a reply), and clicking it pins that step so the trajectory and the icicle jump to it. semantica links every fact to its source node; cctrace's source is the wire request that first sent the turn, matched to the session's spine by content, not index, and anchored on the request's own position in the spine so a repeated prompt or reminder after a compaction resolves to the occurrence nearest that request, not the first one in the session (ctxOriginTurn in src/context.ts - the same content-verified discipline buildSession's attribution uses, because Claude Code repacks ephemeral turns between requests and a bare index drifts). A user-role turn is addressed by the step of the request it entered with on every surface (Trajectory rows say the same turn NN . step N the Context pane says). Envelope rows (system prompt, tool schemas) have no turn and say nothing rather than guessing
- Fixed the exit seal leaving traces plain. A real data dir held 121 plain traces (33 GB) beside 760 archives (3.1 GB) and 58 orphaned seal-*.json jobs: the detached helper 0.42.0 hands the merge + archive to lives exactly as long as the terminal or container the run lived in, and that routinely goes away within a minute of the prompt returning - while the helper was still in the merge scan, which came BEFORE the archive and decompresses every archive in the project dir looking for the session ids (96 s measured on a 73-file dir). Three changes: the run's own trace is archived FIRST (one streamed pass, seconds - the space win is banked before the slow part starts; the merge reads the fresh .zst like any source); the helper is spawned in its own session (setsid), so closing the terminal that ran the session no longer kills it, and it heartbeats its job file while it works; and orphaned jobs are recovered - a live run's startup re-spawns every job idle over 10 minutes (folded per project dir so N orphans cost one scan, one helper working through them in order, three attempts and then it gives up out loud), cctrace compress --yes finishes them inline before planning, and a killed archive's .tmp is swept after an hour. cctrace compress --all on that data dir: 35 GB -> 7.1 GB in 48 s
- Changed the merge scan to stream. planMerge decoded each archive into one string to look for session ids; past ~2 GB decoded that is not representable, and a 199 MB archive of a multi-GB trace killed the process with nothing to catch. The scan now streams line by line (traceLines), measures what each source decodes to, and BLOCKS a session whose source exceeds a 1 GiB in-memory union cap, with the reason, instead of attempting it - its files stay as they are, and every reader already follows a session across files. cctrace merge gets the same guard (the over-cap file is named)
- Added the Context view — a third page tab (#/context) answering "what is the model actually carrying around, and what is eating the window", request by request. Borrowed from dsh-context's design (context stats, composition bar, per-request history chart, events, per-step decomposition) with cctrace's structural advantage: every captured request body IS the fully assembled context, so per-step composition is exact and every step is anchored to the provider-reported prompt tokens from the same wire pair.
